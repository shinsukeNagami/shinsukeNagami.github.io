About紹介

I am a licensed speech-language-hearing therapist (ST) and Professor at the School of Rehabilitation Sciences, Health Sciences University of Hokkaido (Ph.D. in Medicine, 2021). My research integrates natural language processing built on large language models (LLMs) and phonetics with acoustic analysis and respiration-centered biosignal processing. Grounded in psychological research methodology and a commitment to reproducibility, I develop assessment and treatment technologies for dysphagia rehabilitation involving electrical stimulation, acoustic analysis, postural control, and ultrasound imaging. I am also interested in articulatory development in children and attention, and in organizing LLM-based research and clinical workflows.

言語聴覚士。大規模言語モデル（LLM）と音声学を基盤とした自然言語処理、音響学および呼吸を中心とした生体信号解析を専門としています。心理学的な研究手法や再現性への配慮を基底に、これらを統合し、電気・音響解析・姿勢制御・超音波技術に関連した摂食嚥下リハビリテーションのための医療機器開発を推進しています。構音発達や注意機能にも興味があります。近年はLLMを用いたワークフローの整理にも注力しています。

Research Interests研究テーマ

  • Dysphagia rehabilitation — videofluoroscopy (VFSS), swallowing ultrasound, interferential current stimulation, repetitive peripheral magnetic stimulation (rPMS)
  • Motor speech & articulation — oral diadochokinetic (DDK) timing variability, articulation development in children
  • Respiratory–swallow coordination — swallowing physiology in respiratory disease (incl. COPD)
  • Biosignal & acoustic analysis — reproducible measurement pipelines for clinical speech and swallowing data
  • AI for clinical workflows — LLM-based natural language processing, automated analysis of routine clinical examinations
  • Medical device development — assessment and treatment systems for swallowing rehabilitation

Lab & Graduate Students研究室・大学院生

As Principal Investigator, I lead a research group at the School of Rehabilitation Sciences, Health Sciences University of Hokkaido. The lab currently hosts graduate students conducting clinical research in swallowing and speech sciences:

  • Doctoral student (D2) — respiratory–swallow coordination and cough function in older adults
  • Master's student (M1) — peripheral electrical stimulation and elicitation of the swallowing reflex
  • Alumni — graduates whose research addressed swallowing and posture, and swallowing ultrasonography; both remain active in clinical and research careers

Graduate students present at the university's research progress meetings each semester. We also collaborate with engineering and clinical research groups across Japan on medical device development and AI-assisted analysis of clinical data.

Featured: Our lab was introduced in the newsletter of the Japan Geriatric Therapy Society — JGTS News Letter No. 08 (June 2026) (PDF), lab profile “研究室紹介：永見研究室” with photographs of lab members and collaborators. I also serve on the society's Public Relations Committee.
